Culling and the frame

LOD selection

A mesh with levels of detail (LODs) has several versions, from full detail to very coarse. Each frame, the renderer picks one level per object. The goal: the coarsest level that still looks the same as the full one. This page explains how to decide that, from simple distance rules to the screen-space error, and how to hide the moment a level changes. The last part shows how RASM does it. How the levels are made is in Mesh simplification.

In short

  • Each level has a geometric error e: how far its surface may be from the full mesh, in the mesh's own units.
  • Project that error to the screen: pixels = e × s × P11 × H / 2 / w, with the object's scale s, the projection's P11, the screen height H and the view depth w.
  • Pick the coarsest level whose error stays under a limit, usually about 1 pixel.
  • Use the view depth (along the view direction), not the straight distance, and the bounding sphere's nearest point. Both keep the choice conservative.
  • An orthographic camera has no depth division: the level only changes with the zoom.
  • A level change can pop. Hysteresis, cross-fading and a small pixel limit hide it.

2Distance thresholds

The simplest rule: an artist sets a distance per level. Closer than 10 m: LOD 0. 10 to 25 m: LOD 1. And so on.

A better variant uses the object's size on screen instead of its distance: the projected height of its bounding sphere, as a fraction of the screen. Unity's LOD Group and Unreal's static mesh LODs both work this way. It fixes the resolution, field-of-view and scale problems, because they all change the size on screen. It still needs a threshold per mesh, because one mesh's LOD 1 may be much worse than another's.

3The screen-space error

The simplifier already knows how bad each level is: it reports a geometric error e, the largest distance between the level's surface and the full mesh, in the mesh's own units (see Mesh simplification). If we turn that distance into pixels, one rule works for every mesh:

The rule

Pick the coarsest level whose error, seen from the camera, is at most τ pixels. With τ = 1, the difference to the full mesh is never more than one pixel.

4From an error in metres to pixels

How big is a length L on screen, when it stands at right angles to the view direction, at view depth w? Look at the camera from the side:

Step by step, for a perspective projection matrix P:

1. clip space:   y_clip = P11 · y_view,   w_clip = w (the view depth)
2. NDC:          y_ndc  = P11 · y_view / w
3. a length L:   Δy_ndc = P11 · L / w
4. to pixels:    2 NDC units = H pixels, so 1 NDC unit = H / 2 pixels
5. so:           pixels = L · P11 · H / (2 · w)

Here P11 is the matrix entry at row 1, column 1 (counting from 0). For a perspective projection with a vertical field of view θ, P11 = 1 / tan(θ / 2): 1.73 for 60°. The error is stored in the mesh's units, and the object's world matrix can scale it by s, so L = e × s:

pixels = e × s × P11 × H / 2 / w

Only w changes per object, and the levels only change e. So the cheap way is to turn the rule around once per object, and compare each level's error with a limit in mesh units:

pixelsPerUnit = P11 × H / 2 / w
eMax          = τ / (pixelsPerUnit × s)
level         = the last level with e ≤ eMax

The horizontal direction gives the same answer: P00 is P11 divided by the aspect ratio, and the screen is wider by the same ratio.

5Depth, not distance

In the picture, the object is 30 m away, 40° off the centre, so its depth is 30 × cos 40° = 23 m. Dividing by 30 instead of 23 makes its error look 23% smaller than it really is. Near the edges of a wide view, a distance-based rule picks levels that are too coarse. The depth is also cheaper: it is the w of the point in clip space, one row of the matrix.

6Staying conservative

Like culling, LOD selection should only err on the safe side: it may pick a level finer than needed (that costs a little time), never a coarser one (that shows). Each input has a safe choice:

InputSafe choiceWhy
Depthw − r, the nearest point of the bounding sphereThe error can be anywhere on the object. The nearest point shows it biggest.
Camera near or insidew − r ≤ 0 → LOD 0The formula divides by zero or flips sign there.
Scalethe world matrix's largest stretchA non-uniform scale stretches the error most along one axis. The same bound grows the bounding sphere for culling (see Frustum culling).
Error of a levelan upper bound, e.g. the sum of the steps' errorsA smaller number than the truth would show the difference.
Direction of the errorassume it is at right angles to the viewAn error along the view direction looks smaller on screen, but we do not know its direction.

7Worked example

A 60° vertical field of view (P11 = 1.732), a screen 1080 pixels high, a limit τ = 1 pixel. So at depth w, one metre is 1.732 × 1080 / 2 / w = 935.3 / w pixels. A mesh in metres, scale 1, with a bounding sphere of radius 1 m, and these levels:

LevelError eUnder 1 px from depth w − r = e × 935.3
LOD 00always
LOD 10.005 m4.7 m
LOD 20.012 m11.2 m
LOD 30.03 m28.1 m
LOD 40.08 m74.8 m

Now move the object away. Click through (or use the arrow keys). The bars are each level's error on screen; the limit is the dashed line.

1. The sphere at 5 m: LOD 0

Close up, even LOD 1 would move the surface by more than a pixel. The full mesh is drawn.

2. The sphere at 20 m: LOD 2

LOD 1 and 2 stay under a pixel; LOD 3 would show 1.5 pixels. LOD 2 draws a quarter of the triangles.

3. The sphere at 60 m: LOD 3

Three levels are under the limit now.

4. The sphere at 200 m: LOD 4

Every level is under a pixel. The coarsest one is drawn: about 1/16 of the triangles.

A 4K screen

H = 2160 doubles every pixel value, so every switch depth doubles too: LOD 1 from 9.4 m, LOD 4 from 150 m. A sharper screen keeps the detail longer, with no new numbers.

Zooming in

A 20° field of view gives P11 = 5.67, 3.27 times more. The switch depths grow by the same factor, so a zoomed-in far object gets its full detail back.

8The orthographic case

orthographic:  P11 = 2 / (top − bottom),  w = 1
pixels = e × s × P11 × H / 2 = e × s × H / (top − bottom)

The depth drops out. A view that shows 40 m from top to bottom on a 1080-pixel screen has 27 pixels per metre, near or far. The level changes only when the view zooms (changes top − bottom). This is the common case for map engines and CAD views. A matrix tells which case it is: a perspective matrix has 0 in its bottom-right corner, an orthographic one has 1.

9Popping, and how to hide it

When an object switches levels, its shape changes in one frame. If the change is big enough to see, it pops. With τ = 1 pixel the change is at most a pixel, which is hard to see on a moving image. A larger τ (to save more triangles) makes popping visible, and then one of these helps:

Hysteresis

Cross-fading and dithering

For a short time after a switch, draw both levels and blend from the old to the new. Real alpha blending needs sorting and has problems with depth. So engines use dithering (screen-door transparency): each level draws only some pixels, chosen by a fixed pattern, and the two patterns fill the screen together. Over a few frames, more pixels go to the new level.

TechniqueHidesCost
A small limit (τ ≈ 1 px)almost everythingfewer triangles saved
Hysteresisflicker at a thresholdone remembered level per object
Dithered cross-fadethe pop itselftwo draws during the fade, a timer per object, a dither pattern
Geomorphingthe pop itself, smoothlyvertices move from their fine to their coarse position over time; needs to know where each vertex goes (progressive meshes, terrain)

10Variants

VariantIdea
Quality settingScale τ: 2 pixels on a low setting, 0.5 on a high one.
Triangle budgetRaise τ until the scene fits a fixed number of triangles. Keeps the frame time steady, at a varying quality.
Per-cluster levelsSplit a mesh into small clusters and pick a level per cluster, not per object, from a hierarchy of clusters. One big object (a terrain, a building) then has fine detail near the camera and coarse detail far away. Brian Karis's Nanite talk (2021) describes one such system.
Other viewsShadow maps and reflections have their own cameras. Using the main camera's level for them avoids a shadow whose shape differs from the object.

11In RASM

RASM picks a level on the GPU, inside the culling pass, for every draw that passed the frustum and occlusion tests. It uses the screen-space error rule of this page, with τ = EngineConfig::lodErrorPixels (1 pixel by default). The levels and their errors come from Mesh simplification.

float pixelsPerUnit = abs(scene->projection[1][1]) * 0.5 * float(scene->viewportHeight);
if (scene->projection[3][3] == 0.0)
{
    float4 clip = mul(scene->projection, mul(scene->view, float4(sphere.center, 1.0)));
    float depth = clip.w - sphere.radius;
    if (depth <= 0.0)
        return 0;
    pixelsPerUnit /= depth;
}
float limit = scene->lodErrorPixels / (pixelsPerUnit * sphere.scale);

The core of selectLod, shortened. Then it loops over the levels and keeps the last one with lods[i].error <= limit.

Keep these true
  • Stay conservative: the nearest point of the sphere, the largest stretch, and an error that is an upper bound. A tighter number shows the difference.
  • A level's error never gets smaller from one level to the next, and LOD 0's is 0.
  • The level goes above the slot in cullSlotsPtr (slot | lod << CULL_LOD_SHIFT), so a group's slot must stay under 216; it is at most 63.
  • The CPU draw list keeps LOD 0's range; only writeMain writes a coarser one, into the visible draws.
  • SceneData and GeometryData are CPU↔GPU structs: a change follows the struct contract.
Not done yet

No hysteresis or cross-fade, no quality scale beyond lodErrorPixels, no triangle budget, no per-cluster levels. Levels are always in GPU memory: there is no streaming of fine levels for near objects only (D4).
