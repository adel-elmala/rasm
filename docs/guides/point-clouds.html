Drawing

Point clouds

A laser scanner (LiDAR) or a photo survey gives millions of points: a position each, often a color or a value such as height or intensity. There are no triangles to draw. This page explains how to draw such a cloud fast: small splats on the screen, compact storage, a cheap level of detail, and colormaps that turn values into colors. The last part shows how RASM does it.

In short

  • Each point is a splat: a small square, disc or sphere on the screen, a few pixels or a few centimetres wide.
  • Points come in chunks of up to 65 536 near each other. A chunk stores each position as three 16-bit steps of its box: 8 bytes a point with a value, 12 with a color.
  • A point is one triangle around its square; the pixel stage cuts it to its shape.
  • The builder puts a chunk's points in an order where every prefix is an even sample. A chunk small on screen draws only its first points: level of detail with no extra data.
  • A colormap turns a value into a color through a range (linear or logarithmic) and a 256-entry texture.

1Splats: what a point looks like

A point has no size. To see it, the renderer draws a small shape at its place, a splat. Three shapes cover most uses.

Square

The cheapest. At 1 or 2 pixels every shape looks like this anyway.

Round

A disc. Its edge is smooth with several samples per pixel.

Sphere

A disc that writes a sphere's depth: overlapping points meet along a curve, as solid balls would.

The size is in pixels (the same on screen at every distance) or in metres (smaller when far away). A splat is never less than one pixel wide, so a far point neither vanishes nor flickers.

2One triangle a point

The usual splat is a quad: two triangles, four corners. But for points of one or two pixels the cost is per point (the corners and the triangle setup), not per pixel. One triangle that holds the square costs one setup instead of two; the pixel stage cuts it back to the square or the disc.

3Storage: 16 bits in a chunk's box

Points near each other share a box. Inside it, a position needs far less than three 32-bit floats: 16 bits per axis is a step of 1/65 535 of the box, 0.5 mm for a 32 m box. A value (height, intensity, temperature) gets 16 bits too, in steps of the chunk's range of values.

The box also gives the chunk's bounding sphere, so a chunk is culled like any object: out of the view, or behind something in the depth of the frame before. The chunk is placed in the world by a transform in doubles, so a cloud far from the origin keeps its precision (large worlds).

4Level of detail by prefix

A chunk far away covers few pixels, and drawing all its points would put many on the same pixel. The cheap fix: draw only its first k points. That works only if the first k points are spread over the whole chunk, for every k. A scanner writes points line by line, and a random shuffle clumps. The builder sorts the points along a Morton curve (a path that visits every small square before the next one), then reads that list in bit-reversed steps.

  1. Quantize the positions to 16 bits in the box.
  2. Interleave the bits of x, y and z: the Morton code. Sort by it.
  3. For i = 0, 1, 2, … take sorted[reverse(i)], the bits of i reversed (0, P/2, P/4, 3P/4, …).
  4. The first 2j points are every P/2j-th point along the curve: one in each region.

The level of detail costs no memory: no second copy of the points, no tree. Its limit is that a chunk is one unit: a very large chunk near the camera draws all of its points even where part of it is far. Small chunks (tens of metres) keep that error small; a tree of chunks for whole datasets is the next step.

5How many points a chunk keeps

The chunk keeps enough points to cover its square on screen a few times. With D the diameter of its sphere on screen and a a point's area in pixels:

k = min(N, ⌈density × D2 / a⌉)

a = s2 (square), or π/4 × s2 (round, sphere)

D
pixels per unit at the sphere's nearest point × its diameter
s
the point's size in pixels, at least 1; a size in metres is converted at the same depth, so it cancels the distance
density
how many times the points should cover the chunk's square; 0 keeps them all

Measured on a synthetic scan (4 M points, chunks of 32 m, round points of 2 pixels, a top view at 4× MSAA, RTX 3060):

The π/4 matters: a disc covers less than its square. Without it, a density of 1 keeps as many points as 0.785 does with it, and leaves 1.3 % of the ground empty.

6Colormaps: values to colors

A value becomes a color in two steps: the range turns it into t from 0 to 1, and a 256 × 1 texture turns t into a color, read with linear filtering between its entries.

Perceptually even

viridis, magma, inferno, plasma and cividis change lightness evenly, so equal steps of the value look like equal steps. cividis also reads for color-blind viewers.

Turbo

A smooth rainbow: more distinct hues, good for heights, but its lightness goes up and down.

Gray

Lightness only, for intensity scans.

7Anti-aliasing and motion

MSAA: coverage from the distance

The shader turns the distance to the disc's edge into a coverage (alpha-to-coverage): half the samples of an edge pixel, a smooth rim.

TAA: the centre's motion

The projection's jitter moves the splat; every pixel of it writes the motion of the point's centre, so the history follows the point.

8Pitfalls

A far chunk is a stripe or a corner of itself
Its points are in scan order, so a prefix is a part. Build the chunk in a Morton order read in bit-reversed steps.
Holes in a surface at a distance
Too few points for the chunk's size on screen. Raise the density, or the point size.
Holes close to the camera
The data is that sparse: every point is drawn. Use a size in metres about the points' spacing.
Round points vanish at 1 or 2 pixels
A disc that small misses the pixel centres it covers. Draw points under 3 pixels square.
Steps in the positions far from the origin
The points were floats in world space. Keep them as small offsets in their chunk, and the chunk's place in doubles.
Colors look washed out or too dark
The colormap's entries are sRGB; store them in an sRGB texture so the read gives linear color.

9In RASM

A chunk is a mesh made by buildPointCloudChunk, on any thread, and uploaded by Renderer::createMesh. An entity draws it with a MaterialType::POINTS material, whose PointMaterial gives the size, the shape, the colors and the density. The entity's transform places the chunk; culling, render space far from the origin and picking work as for any entity.

Rules to keep

A chunk's order is part of its data: every prefix must stay an even sample. The cull pass decides each chunk's count in selectPointCount only. PointData and PointChunkHeader are kept in step on both sides (struct layout).
